Welcome

Creative Rest

This is your personal map of things that restore you. No productivity goals, no milestones to hit — just a collection of doorways into worlds worth exploring. Wander in whenever you feel like it.

The thread through all of it: making things that respond to the world around them. A tincture shaped by the harvest. A walk shaped by weather. A visual shaped by sound. The practice is receptivity — quiet enough to notice what the world is doing, and then move with it.

Herbalism

Making things with plants — teas you brew to feel better, salves you make with your hands, tinctures aging on the shelf. This is old knowledge worth learning slowly.

Tools & Supplies

Kitchen & Prep
Double boiler, glass jars (mason jars in multiple sizes), cheesecloth or muslin, mortar & pestle, digital scale
Tincture Making
Amber dropper bottles, high-proof alcohol (vodka or Everclear), labels, a dark cupboard
Salves & Creams
Beeswax, carrier oils (jojoba, olive, coconut), shea butter, tins or small jars, essential oils (optional)
Drying & Storage
Drying rack or hanging bundles, paper bags, airtight containers, away from light and heat

Urban Beekeeping

City hives, rooftop bees, balcony pollinator gardens. Learning to live alongside bees is its own quiet philosophy.

Tools to Know About

Hive types
Langstroth (most common), Warré (natural/low intervention), Top Bar (horizontal, gentler)
Protective gear
Veil, gloves, bee suit — start with a full suit until you're comfortable
Smoker
Calms bees during inspections. Fuel: pine needles, burlap, wood chips
Hive tool
The essential pry bar of beekeeping. Always keep it clean

Pollinator Tending

Noticing and nurturing the small flying creatures that hold ecosystems together. Mostly this means planting things and paying attention.

What Pollinators Need

Food
Native flowering plants blooming across the whole season — not just summer
Water
A shallow dish with pebbles so small insects can land safely
Shelter
Leaf litter, hollow stems, bare soil patches, small bee hotels
No pesticides
Even "organic" options can harm pollinators — choose carefully

Networks & Resources

iNaturalist (inaturalist.org)
Identify and log what you observe. Contributes to real science.
Pollinator Partnership Canada (pollinator.ca)
Planting guides by region, community programs

Classic BC alpine. 18km return. Worth every step in summer.

Year-round access; great for short outings or longer summit hikes.

Tea

Tea Ceremony

Tea as a whole practice — gongfu, chado, British high tea, Moroccan mint. Each tradition is a world. A tea sommelier sees them all without hierarchy.

Tools

Gongfu setup
Gaiwan or small teapot, fairness pitcher (chahai), small cups, tea tray or drip tray, kettle with temperature control
Tasting
White porcelain tasting cups (show colour well), tea journal, aroma cup (wenxiangbei)
General
Filtered water (matters more than most equipment), scale for measuring, timer

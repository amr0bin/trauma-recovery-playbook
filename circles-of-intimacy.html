Reference · relational boundaries

Circles of intimacy

A working map of who sits where, what I share at each distance, and what a relationship can hold in both directions. Select any ring or ladder step to cross-reference the two.

Honesty and disclosure are separate Honesty is not saying false things. Disclosure is how many true things I say. A correctly sized answer is not a lie.
Depth is earned, not granted Closeness is built through reciprocal disclosure and reciprocal curiosity over time. Sharing past someone's level does not create a container; it removes one.
Placement follows behaviour A person's wish for closeness is not a claim on a circle. What they have done, repeatedly, sets the position.
Care, capacity and safety are three variables How much someone loves me, how much room they have for my complexity, and how safe it is to be exposed with them are independent. Decades and affection settle none of the three.
The circles

Centre outward. Each ring names who belongs there, the setting I see them in, my disclosure ceiling, and the boundary available to me.

Centre is me. Off-map sits outside the system entirely.
The disclosure ladder

Six levels of personal information. Selecting a level shows every circle that reaches it. Selecting a circle above marks every level it receives.

Circles reaching this level

Circle 5 is the exception: the ceiling is not fixed. I go one level above whatever they last offered, and only after they have offered.

Three reads

Separate questions with separate answers. Read them in this order; the old mistake was reading care first and letting it answer the other two.

Read one
Care
“How much does this person care about me?”
Tells me
Something real about them, and what the loss would cost me.
Licenses
Nothing. It does not raise a ceiling, excuse a breach, or oblige me to keep offering what isn't received.
Read two
Capacity
“What happens when I am complicated, hurt, disagreeing, or inconvenient?”
Tells me
What I bring, and how often.
Doesn't tell me
Whether they love me, or whether they are safe. Curiosity is not the same as trustworthiness.
Read three
Safety
“What happens to what I hand over, and to a no?”
Tells me
My ceiling. This read outranks the other two.
Doesn't tell me
How warm they are. Warmth and safety are unrelated.

Safety sets the ceiling. Capacity sets what I bring. Care sets neither — it only sets what I would grieve.

Where the reads conflict, the lowest one governs. High care never overrides low safety; high capacity never overrides low safety.

Read two — the capacity bands

The circle sets how much I say. Capacity sets what I bring, and how often. These bands describe behaviour under complexity — not affection, and not trustworthiness.

Reciprocal
“I see it differently. Help me understand how you got there.”
Bring complexity. Disagree out loud. Ask for repair and expect a hearing. This is where levels 5 and 6 actually live.
Ceiling: as placed
Intermittent
“I'm drowning right now. I love you, and I can't get into this today.”
Bring complexity when the bandwidth is there; give space without reassessing the relationship. The test is not constant availability — it is whether they move toward me when they can.
Ceiling: as placed, timed
Limited
“I love you. I don't really go there.”
Enjoy what is genuinely on offer — history, humour, affection, practical help. Stop opening the door and waiting. No grievance required.
Ceiling: level 3–4
Managing
“You shouldn't be feeling that. Your reading is wrong. Let's move on.”
Bring no hurt, no disagreement, no repair. Answer briefly and hand the turn back. Closeness elsewhere does not raise this ceiling.
Ceiling: level 2–3

Limited is not managing. Limited declines the conversation. Managing corrects my experience, decides what I meant, or requires me to be uncomplicated so they stay comfortable.

I do not need to establish intent or prove malice. The pattern is enough to set the ceiling, and the ceiling is a placement decision, not a verdict on the person.

Read three — the safety states

Three states. This read governs: it can lower a ceiling that care and capacity would otherwise raise.

Safe
“What I gave them stayed with them. The no was taken the first time.”
Ceiling as placed. Exposure is possible here — being unfinished, needing something, being wrong out loud. Demonstrated across cost, not stated as reassurance.
Ceiling: as placed
Unproven
“Nothing has gone wrong. Nothing has been tested either.”
Matched disclosure only — one level above what they last offered. Not a deficit. The default for anyone new, however good the chemistry.
Ceiling: matched
Unsafe
“It came back to me. Or the no had to be said three times.”
Ceiling drops to level 1–2 immediately, whatever the warmth, history, or curiosity. No hearing required, no explanation owed.
Ceiling: level 1–2
When the reads disagree

The combinations that used to confuse me. Naming the pattern is what makes the response obvious.

What I observeThe readsWhat I do
Loves me enormously, no room for my complexity Care high · capacity limited · safety fine Keep the affection and take the complexity elsewhere. Grieve the gap without prosecuting them for it.
Sharp and genuinely curious, but what I share comes back at me Capacity high · safety low Ideas yes, interior no. Safety governs, so the ceiling sits at 2–3 no matter how good the conversation is.
Warm and affectionate, repeatedly past my no Care high · safety low Warmth does not restore access. Placement moves outward on the behaviour, not on how the warmth feels.
Little bandwidth, entirely trustworthy Capacity intermittent · safety high Placement holds. Ask less often, not less deeply. Bandwidth is not a character read.
Curious about me and safe with what I give Capacity reciprocal · safety high This is where the scarce energy goes. Levels 5 and 6 live here, and I can be the one who initiates.
Movement between circles
Inward — slow, on evidence
  • They reciprocate disclosure at a similar depth.
  • They hold what I gave them without repeating or using it.
  • They behave well when it costs them something — when I am inconvenient, when I say no, when I am not doing well.
  • They are curious about me when I am hard to understand, not only when I am easy.
  • The last two are the test. Many people are warm to a version of me that asks for nothing, and many are comfortable with my complexity only while I am holding theirs.
Outward — fast, no hearing required
  • One breach of confidence moves a person out.
  • Repeated pressure past a stated no moves a person out.
  • I owe no notice, explanation, or final conversation.
  • Demotion is a placement decision, not a verdict on their character.
The intermittent pattern

Someone hostile three quarters of the time and warm the rest is not ambivalent. This is an intermittent pattern, and it holds attention more firmly than consistent unkindness because the unpredictable warmth keeps me reassessing.

Placement: Circle 4 at most. Defined context only. Ceiling level 3.

Method: fix the placement for a set period and stop recalculating per interaction. Genuine change would be evident across twelve consistent months without my monitoring for it.

The read, after an interaction

Five questions, tagged by which read they answer. They set the band, not my feelings about the person.

CapacityDid I leave feeling understood, or managed?Managed is a placement fact, not an accusation.
CapacityWhen I brought something complicated, did they move toward it?Once is chance. A pattern is the answer.
CapacityCould either of us say “I might be wrong”?What I want is mutual reality-testing, not agreement.
SafetyDid I shrink to keep the peace?If yes, the ceiling I am using is too high for this person.
CareWas I explaining why I deserve to be understood?That is an audition. The move is to stop it, not to win it.
My part
What I stop doing
  • I stop teaching people how to be curious about me. I watch whether they already come toward me.
  • I stop interpreting someone's inner world for them. If I have a thought: “I wonder if this is happening — does that land, or am I missing you?”
  • I stop mothering adults who are managing me in return. That over-functioning is my behaviour to change, not theirs.
  • I stop carrying a whole rupture alone. Owning my part is not owning the break.
  • I stop reading silence as an instruction. Their withdrawal belongs to them.
What stays
  • I still want to know when I have hurt someone, misread something, or been unfair.
  • Criticism is information, not a verdict. I can investigate it without accepting it.
  • Someone's discomfort is not proof that I was wrong.
  • I apologise for behaviour, not for having an experience, a value, or a limit.
  • I keep the desire for mutuality. I only change where I spend it.
Repair has a stopping point

Done means: I named my actual part, apologised for that part only, and offered what was genuinely asked for.

After that, further explaining, resourcing, or reassuring is not repair — it is pursuit. Repair needs two people willing to engage; I cannot perform it single-handedly.

If they come back, I can listen. If they don't, that is information about the relationship's capacity, not a verdict on me.

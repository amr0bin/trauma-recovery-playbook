Overview · what this is

Understanding a nervous system as part of an ordinary life

A plain-language, neutral resource for understanding how a nervous system works, how it carries its history, and how I can care for mine. Its aim is literacy — a working understanding I can use — rather than treatment.

A living companion for this work — not a picture of a nervous system, but a place to sit beside one. Set the weather you're in, or touch a part of the whole to feel how it moves with the rest. Nothing here needs answering; the questions are the practice.

The weather in me
Touch a part of the whole

This isn't a diagram to finish. Come back to it — set the weather, sit with one part, notice what moves. The whole is always more than its pieces, and so are you.

The north star — four aims

Everything here points toward restoring four qualities to a system that, at some point, learned to live without them.

Safety
Enough predictability that the body can stop bracing.
Flexibility
The capacity to shift states rather than getting stuck.
Agency
Acting from choice, at my own pace, not from survival.
Connection
Steadying in trusted company, and being able to ask.

What this is not
  • Not therapy, diagnosis, or crisis care, and not a substitute for professional support.
  • Not a protocol promising a fixed result. A nervous system is an n-of-1; general information becomes useful only once I test it against my own experience.
  • Not a project in self-improvement. The posture is closer to study than to repair — understanding a system, not correcting a faulty one.
A note on the science

Some frameworks this field leans on — polyvagal theory among them — are influential and clinically useful, and also genuinely contested in the research literature. Where that is true, the guide says so rather than presenting a tidy consensus that does not exist. Confidence itself is treated as information worth being honest about.

Overview · how I use it

A short contract about pace and sequence

Two ideas run underneath everything here: safety comes before insight, and capacity comes before intensity. When the guide puts things in an order, this is the reason.

Why the order matters

A striking realisation is worth little if reaching it leaves me flooded. A demanding practice is premature while I lack the reserves to recover from it. So the sequencing is protective, not arbitrary: each step is meant to leave me steady enough to take the next.

The contract
01
Move at my own paceNothing here needs finishing today. A page can be closed mid-sentence and returned to with more capacity later.
02
Rising activation is informationIf a section lifts my heart rate or tightens my chest, that reaction marks where my edge sits right now. Slowing down is the intended response; pushing through is not.
03
Stopping is a skillEnding with energy still in reserve is a capacity in its own right, and it protects every other skill I am trying to build.
04
Start anywhereThere is a suggested route, but it is one path among several. Curiosity guides better than obligation.

Foundations · the terrain

Ten bodies of knowledge, written as description

The terrain this guide rests on. These are not chapters to memorise but the background that makes the practical skills make sense — the map beneath the walking. Each closes with a single sentence worth leaving with.

Body & state

How the system runs moment to moment, and how I come to notice it.

1
Autonomic nervous system physiology contested in placesThe load-bearing wall. The autonomic nervous system governs the background functions I rarely choose — heart rate, breath, digestion, alertness — and moves between rough states of activation, settledness, and shutdown. Popular models such as polyvagal theory give these states an accessible vocabulary and see wide clinical use; parts of the theory are also disputed by researchers. The guide holds both facts together.Leave with: my body is always running a state beneath my thoughts, and that state can change.
3
Interoception & body literacyThe learnable sense of my own internal condition — the felt read on tension, temperature, breath, hunger, energy. Every other skill rests on it, since I cannot tend a state I cannot feel. This is basic literacy anyone can develop, not a specialised therapy.Leave with: noticing what my body is doing is a skill that sharpens with practice.
4
Everyday physiology — sleep, light, movement, foodSleep, circadian rhythm, movement, and nourishment form the universal substrate of nervous system function. Deliberately de-medicalised: these levers matter for everyone, and small steadiness here supports everything above it.Leave with: the plainest inputs — when I sleep, see daylight, move, and eat — shape how regulated I feel.
Change & time

Why a nervous system is never fixed in place.

2
Neuroplasticity & experience-dependent changeEvery nervous system is continuously reshaped by what it lives through. Plasticity is not a treatment mechanism switched on during recovery; it is an ordinary condition of being alive. The same capacity that let hard experience leave its mark lets new experience gradually revise it.Leave with: my nervous system was shaped by experience, so further experience can keep shaping it.
6
Developmental & lifespan perspectiveNervous systems differ in childhood, adulthood, and later life, and both injury and adaptation unfold across the whole arc of a lifespan. Placing my own history on that arc normalises it as a human pattern moving through time rather than a personal anomaly.Leave with: a nervous system keeps developing at every age — no window closes on change.
People & meaning

How others, and the words I choose, steady or unsettle a system.

5
Attachment & co-regulation across a lifespanHumans settle each other's physiology; a calm presence can lower another person's arousal without a word. Co-regulation is ordinary interdependence, the way bodies have always helped one another find steadiness — a mechanism of regulation rather than a reward earned at the end.Leave with: other nervous systems help regulate mine, and needing that is design, not weakness.
9
Language & meaning-makingHow people narrate, name, and integrate their history — in plain, non-clinical words — shapes how that history sits in the body. A private vocabulary that belongs to me, rather than one borrowed wholesale from a diagnostic manual, keeps the experience livable.Leave with: the words I use for what happened are part of how I carry it.
Framing & honesty

The wider context a neutral resource has to hold about itself.

7
"Living-with" frameworksDisability studies, chronic-illness wisdom, and pacing offer a language of accommodation rather than cure — integrating a body's real limits and rhythms into an ordinary life instead of waiting for that life to begin once the limits are gone. Pacing becomes a skill, not a resignation.Leave with: living well with a sensitive system is a legitimate aim, not a consolation prize.
8
History & cultural framingThe very concepts of "trauma" and "nervous system injury" have a history — named, contested, and reframed across eras, with debates about medicalisation still open. A neutral resource situates its own vocabulary rather than treating today's terms as timeless.Leave with: the words this field uses are recent and evolving, not fixed truths.
10
Epistemic honesty & the contested scienceHow confidently a claim can be made is itself a capacity. This foundation marks where evidence is strong, where it is emerging, and where reasonable experts still disagree. Neutrality here is an active commitment — the willingness to say "we don't fully know" out loud.Leave with: a trustworthy guide marks its own uncertainty instead of hiding it.
capacities · the spine

Nine capabilities, in the order a system can bear them

The foundations describe the terrain; the capacities are what I can do and embody within it. Knowledge is "I understand what the vagus nerve does." capacity is "I can notice when I have tipped into shutdown, and gently come back." This guide grows the second, borrowing from the first only as much as the second requires.

The shape at a glance

A spine, not a checklist. The order reflects what a system can carry, not a ranking of worth.

Layer 1
Perceive
Sense my own state — the ground everything else stands on.
Layer 2
Steady
Let the system stop bracing and hold enough safety to grow from.
Layer 3a
Expand
Widen capacity and reclaim range through small, recoverable challenge.
Layer 3b
Connect
Steady in trusted company, and author my own account.
Why this order

Perceiving comes first because I cannot tend a state I cannot sense. Steadying precedes challenge because a system that does not feel safe cannot afford to expand. Reaching for connection asks that some steadiness already be in place. Each layer makes the next one survivable.

The two threads

Running through all four layers are two dispositions rather than stages: self-compassion, which keeps the work from curdling into pressure, and scope awareness, which keeps it safe. They are covered on Two Dispositions.

capacity · layer 1

Perceive — learning to feel my own state

Two capabilities sit at ground zero: sensing internal signals, and recognising which state I am in. I cannot tend what I cannot notice, so everything begins here.

capacity 1
Interoceptive literacy
Noticing internal signals — breath, tension, temperature, energy — without rushing to fix or judge them.
capacity 2
State awareness & self-mapping
Recognising and naming the state I am in, and learning the private signature of each one.
Interoceptive literacy

This is the felt read on my inner condition, sharpened the way any perception is — by returning attention to it, gently and often. The aim is to register, not to interpret or correct; judgement can wait. First there is simply noticing.

Beginner marker

I can answer "what is my body doing right now?" with more than "fine" or "bad" — naming, say, a tight jaw, a shallow breath, and a cool restlessness in the hands.

State awareness & self-mapping

States tend to fall into three rough families: activated (keyed up, fast, braced), settled (open, easy, present), and shut down (flat, distant, heavy). Naming the family helps; learning my own early signals — the specific signs that this is where I am heading — is what makes the awareness usable in real time.

These names are handles, not diagnoses. The point is orientation, not labelling myself.

capacity · layer 2

Steady — the safety layer

Three capabilities let a system stop bracing and hold enough steadiness to grow from: influencing my state gently, building predictable rhythm, and pacing my load.

capacity 3
Self-regulation
Influencing my state from the body up — gently, not by force.
capacity 4
Safety & rhythm
Enough predictability and containment that the system relaxes its guard.
capacity 5
Pacing & load
Budgeting energy and stopping with reserves left — the skill that protects the others.
Self-regulation

Regulation here means influence, not command. States shift most reliably through the body — breath, movement, temperature, posture — rather than by arguing with them from the top down. A workable starting kit is small: one reliable way to ease activation, and one gentle way to lift out of numbness.

Building safety & rhythm

A system that keeps meeting the unpredictable stays braced. Steady sleep and mealtimes, familiar routines, and a contained environment give it permission to lower its guard. Part of this capacity is telling genuine threat apart from mere discomfort or novelty, so the alarm is saved for what warrants it.

Pacing & load management

This is the signature skill of the layer: budgeting energy across a day, reading the early signs of overreach before the crash, and — centrally — stopping with something still in the tank.

The 70% rule

Easing off at around seventy percent of capacity, with energy deliberately left over, protects tomorrow's ability to continue. Emptying the tank each time teaches the system that effort ends in depletion — the opposite of what I am building.

capacity · layer 3

Expand & Connect — where steadiness turns into range

Four capabilities, taken on only once there is safety to spend: widening capacity, testing my own system, steadying with others, and shaping what happened into a story I can live inside.

capacity 6
Capacity-building
Widening the window of tolerance through small doses of challenge paired with real recovery.
capacity 7
Self-experimentation
Turning a question about my own system into a small test, and reading the result as data.
capacity 8
Co-regulation & relational safety
Steadying with trusted others, reading safety accurately, asking for and receiving support.
capacity 9
Meaning & narrative agency
Organising what happened into a livable story, reclaiming authorship and a sense of future.
Capacity-building

Capacity widens the way any capacity does: through challenge small enough to recover from. The working ratio is roughly one to one, a dose of effort matched by genuine restoration. Growth lives in the recovery as much as in the reach; challenge without recovery is only accumulated strain.

Self-experimentation

General advice becomes personal knowledge only once I test it on myself. This capability forms a question about my own system, runs a small low-stakes trial, and reads the outcome as information rather than a verdict. It has its own training ground on Tiny Experiments.

Co-regulation & relational safety

Building on the attachment foundation, this is the practised side of connection: steadying in a trusted presence, reading relational safety accurately, and being able to both ask for and take in support. Because connection functions as a regulating mechanism, it belongs inside the developmental arc rather than after it.

Meaning & narrative agency

The furthest-reaching capability gathers what has happened into an account I can live within, and returns a sense of authorship, dignity, and a future oriented forward rather than organised around survival. This is where the deeper arc of the work settles.

capacities · the two threads

Two dispositions, threaded through everything

Not stages in the spine but threads running through all of it. Together they keep the work both kind and safe.

Self-compassion & non-judgment

The guardrail. A practice must not increase rigidity or perfectionism; if a capacity is being built harshly, it is being built wrong. This works as a test rather than a platitude — when the work starts to feel like another arena for self-criticism, that feeling is the cue to soften the method, not to try harder.

Scope awareness — knowing my limits

The responsibility that keeps a self-directed guide safe. It means recognising what lies beyond self-tending, and treating "hand this to a professional" as a capacity of its own rather than a failure.

Why threads, not steps

These two are never completed and left behind like the layers of the spine. They apply at every layer, on every page, from the first noticing to the deepest meaning-making. A practice built without them can still work mechanically — and quietly do harm.

Practices

Tiny Experiments — studying myself, not fixing myself

A micro-lesson in five phases — Why, See, Try, Share, Reflect — for turning a question about my own system into a small trial I can run. The outcome is not a new habit but a new stance.

Why an experiment, not a goal

A goal points at a destination and quietly asks the body to perform, and for a sensitive system that performance pressure can register as low-grade threat — the very physiology this resource works to soften. A question is safe to hold: it cannot be failed, only learned from.

The five phases
Why
Relevance & a safe postureMy nervous system is an n-of-1; no protocol can be certain what it needs. I already notice, informally, that some mornings leave me steadier — that noticing is experimental thinking without structure. The posture that keeps it safe: I am here to study myself, with a naturalist's curiosity rather than a manager's impatience.
See
Form one small questionI pick a single variable I am curious about — a light, a bedtime, a food, a short walk, a boundary — and phrase it as a question. "What happens to my afternoon energy if I step outside within an hour of waking?" is a question. "I must walk every morning" is a target in disguise.
Try
Run it small, for a set timeI choose a scale so modest it feels almost too easy, and a short, defined window — a few days, not forever. The design should cost me almost nothing, so that running it never becomes another load to recover from.
Share
Tell one trusted person (optional)Saying the experiment aloud to someone safe makes it real without making it heavy. This phase is genuinely optional; connection appears here as support, never as an obligation or an audience to perform for.
Reflect
Read the result as dataAt the window's end I ask what I noticed, not whether I "succeeded." A surprising result is the most useful kind. Then I decide, without pressure, whether the finding is worth keeping, adjusting, or setting down.
The reframe that makes it safe

The goal is understanding, and understanding cannot be gotten wrong. A "failed" experiment that taught me something about my own system did exactly its job. If any phase raises my activation, that too is data — I am free to stop and return with more capacity. Stopping is the skill.

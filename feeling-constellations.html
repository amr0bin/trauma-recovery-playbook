Feeling constellations

Affect at the centre, twelve constellations, fifty-eight named states, an open edge. Built on the Other Bodies deck. Nothing here is aimed at making a feeling stop.

Start at the centre if there is no word yet. Pleasant or unpleasant, high or low energy. That much is always available.

Start at a constellation if the texture is clear but the word is not. Start at the outer ring if a word is already there.

If nothing fits, that is the most useful outcome. It means there is a distinction here that English handed me no word for, and one can be built at the edge.

Seven channels, offered for every constellation. Routes, not instructions. None of them is trying to return me to calm.

The frame comes from Emily and Amelia Nagoski's account of the stress response as a physiological arc that runs whether or not the situation causing it has resolved. Their seven are physical activity, breathing, positive social interaction, laughter, affection, crying, and creative expression.

Three of those need another person. Because I wanted a set that stays entirely within my own control, the social three are replaced here with touch, name, and space, each with its own evidence below. That substitution is mine, not the Nagoskis'.

The seven sit at the constellation level rather than the card level, because action tendency is a family-level property. There is no evidence that betrayal and jealousy need different breathing. Each card adds its own natural body and one line of specificity instead.

Body

Movement gives an activated state somewhere to land. The least controversial item here; physical activity has broad, well-replicated effects on mood and stress physiology.

Nagoski & Nagoski (2019). Levine (1997) on discharge and titration.

Breath

Slower breathing with a lengthened exhale. In a randomised comparison of five-minute daily practices, exhale-emphasised cyclic sighing improved mood and lowered respiratory rate more than mindfulness meditation over a month.

Balban et al. (2023). One trial, 108 participants, short duration.

Touch

Hand on heart, hand on belly, arms around shoulders. In a randomised trial, self-soothing touch lowered cortisol after a lab stressor about as much as being hugged. Heart rate and self-reported stress did not differ, so there is a measurable physiological effect that may not be a felt one.

Dreisoerner et al. (2021). Neff (2003) on self-compassion.

Name

Putting the state into words. Affect labelling reduces amygdala response and raises right ventrolateral prefrontal activity. The direction has replicated well; the mechanism is still argued over.

Lieberman et al. (2007). Brach's RAIN is the practice version.

Page

Writing without an audience. The largest meta-analysis of expressive writing found a real but small average effect, r = .075, across 146 randomised studies, varying a lot by circumstance.

Pennebaker & Beall (1986); Frattaroli (2006). Honest summary: modest, and it depends.

Space

Changing the room, the light, the sightline, or simply orienting. Turning the head and letting the eyes find where I actually am.

Kaplan (1995) on restorative environments. Levine (1997) on orienting. Decent for restoration, weaker for specific protocols.

Make

Expression with no message and no audience. This is the channel Other Bodies is entirely built on, and the deck's own instruction is the best version of it: make something honest, not something good.

Nagoski & Nagoski (2019). Lorde (1977/1984). Robin, Other Bodies (2026).

On not finishing

Any of these can be abandoned partway. Thirty seconds is not a failed attempt. Capacity, rather than intensity of expression, is what the current trauma literature associates with recovery, so going small is not a lesser version of the practice.

See What this is for how confident that claim should be.

English is the narrow part. These are states other languages named and English did not, placed in the constellation each belongs to.

Jackson and colleagues estimated emotion semantics across 2,474 spoken languages using colexification, where a language names two related concepts with one word. They found substantial variation in what emotion words mean between language families, predicted by geographic proximity, with only valence and arousal holding steady throughout.

So a wheel built in one language has a ceiling built into it. These words are here to mark where the ceiling is. Every gloss below is an approximation by definition, some are academically contested, and none of them is a translation.

What this is standing on, and where it is standing on less than it looks.

Reasonably well established

  • Naming a state in words changes measurable brain activity, in the direction of reduced limbic response.
  • Physical activity and lengthened-exhale breathing reliably affect stress physiology.
  • Self-directed touch lowers cortisol after a stressor.
  • Expressive writing has a small positive average effect.
  • Emotional granularity, having more precise words available, is associated with better regulation outcomes.
  • Emotion word meanings vary substantially across languages, with valence and arousal the stable exception.

Contested or unknown

  • Whether there is any set of basic emotions at all. Core affect, primary-process circuits, and constructionism disagree about what the question means.
  • Whether any specific emotion has its own body signature. A meta-analysis of 202 studies found no reliable autonomic fingerprint for discrete categories.
  • Polyvagal theory's physiological premises, which have been directly challenged and defended in the peer-reviewed literature.
  • Whether Panksepp's seven mammalian circuits map onto human felt experience. Most of that evidence is animal.
  • Whether a stress response completes in a definable window. The framing is useful; the timing claims are not established.
  • Whether any of this works for one particular person. That is an n of 1 and no study answers it.

The most important caveat. Every bodily description here is written as commonly reported, never as diagnostic. Siegel and colleagues analysed 202 studies of autonomic activity during induced emotion and found enough variation within each category that the idea of an emotion-specific fingerprint does not hold up.

So if my body does something different from what a panel describes, the panel is wrong about me. Not the other way round.

Why there are no basic emotions at the centre

The centre of this wheel is affect, not an emotion. That is a commitment: valence and arousal are treated as the ingredients, and everything outside the hub is a learned concept rather than a primitive. Which means the constellation ring is not a set of basic emotions. It is the coarsest useful grouping, and it could have been drawn differently.

Three positions are live in the field and they disagree. Russell's core affect says the primitives are two dimensions. Panksepp's affective neuroscience says there are seven subcortical circuits shared across mammals: SEEKING, RAGE, FEAR, LUST, CARE, PANIC/GRIEF, PLAY, mapped by brain stimulation and lesion work, mostly in animals. Barrett's constructionism says there are no basic emotions, only affect plus concepts that are learned and language-specific. None of these hands you a set of words for a wheel, and Panksepp's own list has neither disgust nor shame on it.

An earlier version of this wheel used Gloria Willcox's six. Those were arrived at by taking a folk four and splitting one of them into three, published in 1982 as a communication aid. Good tool, never validated as a taxonomy, and organised by pleasantness, which is why states like ambivalence, nostalgia and awe had nowhere to sit.

Where the twelve come from

Ten of them are the feeling constellations from Other Bodies, kept as written, organised by quality and texture and how a state moves through the body rather than by whether it feels good. That organising principle is closer to how contemporary affective science groups things than any six-emotion wheel is.

Two were added here and are not from the deck. Aversive, because disgust and contempt appear in nearly every taxonomy, have their own action tendency of expelling rather than attacking or withdrawing, and were absent. Appetitive, because wanting as such had no family; longing sat under Relational and curiosity under Expansive, and neither covers appetite. Neither new constellation has cards yet.

The constellation slices are unequal because the card counts are unequal. That is left visible rather than padded. Some regions of feeling are more densely named than others, which is true, and which is also Cowen and Keltner's finding about the shape of the space.

What this is not

Not therapy, not diagnosis, not crisis care. The deck says this in its own front matter and it holds here. A wheel cannot tell whether a feeling is proportionate, whether a situation is safe, or whether something needs more attention than one person can give it alone.

If a state stops moving, constant across weeks, arriving without a stimulus, or bringing thoughts of harm, this stops being the right tool. Reaching for a professional is a competency, not a failure.

Everything cited here is independently verifiable. Where a claim rests on a single study, that is said so.
